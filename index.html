FEIE Calculator for U.S. Expats

See What You’d Owe With the Foreign Earned Income Exclusion

This free FEIE calculator works as a quick U.S. expat tax calculator: it estimates the U.S. income tax you’d still owe while living abroad. The Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (FEIE) lets qualifying U.S. citizens and green card holders living abroad exclude up to $132,900 of foreign earned income for 2026, on the return you file in 2027. Answer up to 10 questions to see whether it applies to you, a range for any U.S. tax left over, and whether the Foreign Tax Credit may suit you better.

How This FEIE Calculator Works

The calculator uses the IRS figures for the 2026 tax year. The FEIE takes your foreign pay out of U.S. income tax, up to the yearly limit. Any pay above the limit is taxed at the rate it would face if nothing were excluded, which is the IRS stacking rule. If you pay foreign income tax, the Foreign Tax Credit can lower what’s left.

Frequently Asked Questions

How Accurate Is This FEIE Calculator?

It uses the IRS figures for 2026 and the same stacking rule the IRS uses, so the range is a reliable starting point. It assumes the standard deduction and doesn’t cover housing costs, every credit or state tax, so a Greenback accountant confirms the final number on your return.

Why Does the Calculator Show a Range Instead of One Number?

You pick an income band rather than an exact figure, so the calculator shows the tax at the low and high end of that band, rounded to the nearest $500. If your income sits near the middle of the band, your tax will usually land near the middle of the range.

Do I Still File a U.S. Return if the Calculator Shows $0?

Yes. The FEIE isn’t automatic. You claim it on Form 2555 with your U.S. return, even when you owe nothing. U.S. citizens living abroad get an automatic extension to June 15 to file, though interest on any tax owed still starts on April 15.
